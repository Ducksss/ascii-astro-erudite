I enlisted on 4 July 2023, after working as a software engineer at GovTech. I was comfortable with a laptop and considerably less comfortable with what came next. Even passing the Individual Physical Proficiency Test and qualifying for the two-month reduction in service had surprised me.

My time in the Singapore Armed Forces ended on 5 May 2025. In between, I went through Basic Military Training, Officer Cadet School and Signals Institute, then served as a cyber defence platoon commander. The dates make a tidy sequence, but the memories are much less tidy.

## Basic Military Training

The food was bad and I missed home more than I had expected. Physical fatigue was part of it, but so was losing the small routines I had taken for granted. Familiar food and familiar people suddenly felt very far away, and the remaining weeks seemed long even near the beginning.

Nicholas was my buddy. I got things wrong often enough, and he was patient with me. That is the part of BMT I want to keep in this account, because a lot of what made it bearable came from having someone beside me who looked out for me. After our Passing Out Parade, he went to the Air Force and our paths split.

The surprising exception to my general misery was the Situational Test. I enjoyed camping, gave away my food and got excited about solving problems with the guys around me. I still had heat rash, but for once the discomfort was not the main thing on my mind. I had expected field conditions to be the part I hated most, so liking them caught me off guard.

By the Passing Out Parade, I mostly felt relief.

## Officer Cadet School

Getting selected for Officer Cadet School surprised me. On my first day, I greeted the instructor who would be with me for the next three months as “sergeant”. She was a captain. I had made it into OCS without yet being able to read the ranks properly.

That memory is more useful to me than a polished account of becoming confident. Selection had changed where I was going, but it had not filled in everything I still needed to learn.

Signals Institute came afterwards. The CCNA networking lessons are the part I remember most clearly. Compared with BMT and OCS, it was a quieter stretch, and much of the rest has blurred together.

## After commissioning

I became a cyber defence platoon commander. The role changed what I had to pay attention to, because my own fatigue was no longer the only fatigue that mattered. I was also responsible for other people, their morale and how they experienced the standards we were expected to meet.

I still sometimes wondered what I would have been building if National Service had not interrupted the life I knew. That thought did not disappear after commissioning. It sat alongside the role I actually had, rather than resolving into a neat lesson about being grateful for everything.

When I finished on 5 May 2025, I was glad to have made it through. I remember Nicholas, the field camp I unexpectedly enjoyed and the captain I called sergeant more clearly than any grand conclusion about leadership.
