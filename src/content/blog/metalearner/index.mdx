MetaLearner brings supply-chain forecasting and planning together with ERP data, the operational records a business keeps in its enterprise resource planning system. The frontend I worked on combined a BI dashboard, forecasts, inventory views and a chat panel for asking questions about that data.

Those screens offer different ways into the same information. A dashboard gives an overview, a chart lets you inspect a pattern and a question lets you narrow the scope. Someone opening the product for the first time still needs to choose where to begin.

During my internship, I implemented a guided onboarding flow, an in-product User Guide and a chart gallery. I also explored a redesign in Figma and put together a separate real-time messaging prototype. These were different kinds of work, and the distinction matters when reading the series.

## Choose a part to explore

### [The guided tour and the first useful question](/blog/metalearner/onboarding)

The tour introduces the dashboard, while four guide categories cover reading it, exploring details, asking for data and changing settings. This chapter looks at where a short introduction should stop and how a specific prompt gives a conversation a useful starting point.

### [Inspecting a time series with zoom and hover](/blog/metalearner/charts)

The chart gallery contains nine demos across six categories, with English, Spanish and Portuguese content. The stock-chart example shows how range controls, hover inspection and a changing vertical scale affect what you can read from a line.

### [Desktop, chat and dashboard tradeoffs in Figma](/blog/metalearner/redesign)

The design exploration started with mobile layouts and moved towards the desktop workflow after team review. The interesting part is deciding how chat, KPI cards, alerts and graphs share a screen, rather than choosing a new coat of paint.

### [Passing a message between backend replicas](/blog/metalearner/handoff)

The separate chat prototype uses FastAPI WebSockets, PostgreSQL and Redis pub/sub. This chapter follows a message across two backend processes and explains the difference between storing a message and notifying a connected browser.

The [internship handoff](https://metalearner-internship-logs.vercel.app/) collects the demos and design references. Lim received the chart implementation, and Carl's feedback on the chart tooltips and grouping helped shape the interaction work described here.
